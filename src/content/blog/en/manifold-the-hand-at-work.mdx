<p class="written-by-hands">Hands enter, repeat a gesture, drift out of unison, and meet again. Each movement is both an action and a record of that action. Robots now learn from records like these. My guess, and it is only a guess: the work of human hands earns something like fifteen trillion dollars a year. Recordings of that work are bought for a few dollars an hour, sometimes for nothing, then copied into every machine that follows. Paid once and kept forever, the know-how of the hand is going for pennies on the dollar.</p>

<small class="manifold-credit">The estimate: about a quarter of world labour income goes to manual work, roughly 16 trillion dollars a year (range 12–22 trillion), from <a href="https://rplumber.ilo.org/data/indicator/?id=EMP_TEMP_SEX_OCU_NB_A&ref_area=X01&sex=SEX_T&timefrom=2023&format=.csv">ILO</a> and <a href="https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/WLD/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.CD?format=json&mrv=3">World Bank</a> data. Reported pay for filming hand work to train robots in 2026 runs from nothing to about 15 dollars an hour (<a href="https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/jun/24/indian-factory-workers-told-film-themselves-for-ai-robots">Guardian</a>, <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/26/human-archive-taps-into-indias-services-startups-to-collect-data-for-physical-ai/">TechCrunch</a>, <a href="https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/01/1134863/humanoid-data-training-gig-economy-2026-breakthrough-technology/">MIT Technology Review</a>). Rough figures; corrections welcome.</small>
