*Revised 7 November 2026. Observations below are dated; this is not a live service-status report.*

On 4 November, at 11:29 JST, FabCafe's homepage returned HTTP 503: a maintenance page instead of the usual website. Its notice attributed the precautionary suspension to a critical PHP vulnerability. The same page said its physical locations remained open and events were continuing as scheduled.<sup><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup>

Calling FabCafe “down” would lose the most useful distinction. The website was unavailable at that observation; the operator said the cafés were operating. Neither statement dates the website's outage onset or proves what happened between our checks.

A security response involves several kinds of work: understanding local exposure, correcting advice, helping people act and assigning what remains. Those records matter. **Recovery asks a further question: which function can people use again, for whom, and since when?**

## Useful information gives someone a next step

Debian's xz advisory distinguished stable releases from affected development branches, making a broad warning locally useful.<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup> Apache explicitly explained why earlier Log4j mitigations were insufficient; its archived guidance retained class removal as a fallback, rather than discrediting every remedy.<sup><a href="#ref-4">4</a></sup> These are useful contributions, not measurements of how many users became protected.

Heartbleed supplies stronger action evidence. Researchers sent contacts lists of vulnerable hosts and recovery guidance. After eight days, 39.5% of early-notified contacts had at least one listed host patched, versus 26.8% of those not yet notified.<sup><a href="#ref-5">5</a></sup> That is a measured contact-level difference of 12.7 percentage points. It is neither a percentage of fully secured hosts nor a trial showing that assistance beats a general warning.

May First's PHP notice shows another form of help: it reports patching its shared webservers, including older PHP, and links instructions while encouraging members to change versions.<sup><a href="#ref-6">6</a></sup> Its maintenance commitment is qualified by members' needs and its capability.<sup><a href="#ref-7">7</a></sup> We can describe the help without claiming that every member followed it or that every site was recovered.

## The website and the service can recover separately

We ran a bounded Japan pilot: twelve provider–function pairs across internet access, banking/payments, energy, flights, rail and messaging/comms, for 20 October–3 November. Official news came first; notices guided endpoint checks and archive lookups. Providers without usable evidence stayed in the sample.

Three units yielded scoped historical operator reports: DOCOMO voice services, TEPCO electricity supply in part of Chiba, and JR East train operations on some Chiba lines.<sup><a href="#ref-8">8</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-9">9</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-10">10</a></sup> Nine lacked adequate evidence for the selected function. None of the twelve had an independently verified present function state. **Unknown did not mean unavailable—or normal.** The retained pilot notices did not explicitly establish a PHP link; this is not evidence that PHP caused no disruption.

The function boundary changes the reading. Mitsubishi UFJ NICOS reported returning its members-only WEB login page to its normal presentation from 26 October and resuming some functions. It did not individually name corporate purchasing-card statement retrieval.<sup><a href="#ref-11">11</a></sup> That notice cannot settle whether statements were available, still less whether card payments worked.

Kansai Transmission & Distribution reported suspending website functions, including part of its outage-information service, and restoring them in a same-date notice on 22 October.<sup><a href="#ref-12">12</a></sup> An app was offered as an alternative information channel. These are records of website disruption and recovery, not electricity-supply failure and restoration.

Even a “normal” label needs checking. Some inspected pages contained hidden or default status templates populated by JavaScript. Reading their unrendered HTML as a live all-clear would have created false normality in our results.

## The first archived error is not the start of an outage

For a named endpoint, we look for the last observed success, first observed failure, last observed failure and first observed recovery. Those observations can bound transitions. They do not prove continuous downtime between captures, and a missing notice is not a successful service test.

Operator reports can provide better clocks. DOCOMO describes a scoped voice disruption on 28 October from around 16:30 to around 17:29 JST.<sup><a href="#ref-8">8</a></sup> That supports a reported interval of roughly an hour, not an independently measured 59-minute outage for every subscriber. Its separate Okinawa restoration notice warns that temporary recovery could still leave some areas with difficulty or slower data speeds.<sup><a href="#ref-13">13</a></sup>

Archives helped recover JR East's notices, but not a complete service history. Two sampled pages carried a weather-related train-suspension banner. A separate software-related digital-services notice appeared in the later sample and expressly excluded train-operation effects.<sup><a href="#ref-10">10</a></sup> The observations must not be joined into one PHP outage or one continuous duration.

## Stopping a service does not settle containment

Binance's initial account of its 29 October incident adds a separate example. It says suspending withdrawals and shutting hot-wallet services did not invalidate malicious authorisations already recorded on-chain. The team needed a separate procedure to revoke those permissions.<sup><a href="#ref-14">14</a></sup>

That is Binance's initial account, published on 3 November, not an independent forensic finding. It does not establish a PHP connection or a time when normal customer withdrawal service was restored. The operational distinction is important: a service can be suspended while a compromised capability remains usable. Containment, restoring customer access and establishing security require different evidence. Binance is an illustration separate from the Japan pilot.

## If you run the systems, report the function you checked

Keep the notice you acted on, its version and the time. If advice changes, preserve what it replaced. A current page may no longer show the instructions someone followed earlier.

Find the person who controls each layer before the next warning. The build pipeline, the configuration file, the managed database and the customer's application may sit with different people.

Route a practical obstacle to someone who can address it: a vendor, distributor, colleague or support community. State the boundary of that help rather than assuming a published procedure was used successfully.

Report the checkpoints separately: patch installed, permissions revoked, harmful activity contained, function restored, exceptions remaining. For restoration, name the function, affected users or region, observation time and evidence. A successful webpage load cannot substitute for a transaction or delivery check; neither alone proves that a compromised capability has been removed.

Distinguish a workaround from restoration of the original channel. Say when an outcome is operator-reported, directly observed or still unknown. Missing completion evidence is not proof that no work happened.

## Ask what people can do again

The next useful sentence after “we responded” names something a person can do again. Can they retrieve the statement, reach the service or use the original channel? If we cannot yet answer, that is the next gap to investigate.

## Evidence and scope

The Japan pilot used dated public notices and archival captures for twelve preselected critical provider–function pairs, two per sector, over 20 October–3 November 2026. Three yielded scoped historical reports; nine target functions remained unknown, and all twelve present states were independently unverified. This is a purposeful pilot, not a national sample. The figure's selected illustrations, including FabCafe and Binance, are separate from the pilot roster.

Search coverage, access restrictions, client-rendered pages and incomplete archives limit what can be established. An archive error may belong to the archive rather than the original site. Event, notice, capture and retrieval times are distinct. No end-user transaction, message-delivery or physical-service tests were performed; the records make no current-availability claim.
