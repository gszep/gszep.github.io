*Revised 5 November 2026. Observations below are dated; this is not a live service-status report.*

On 4 November, at 11:29 JST, FabCafe's homepage returned HTTP 503: a maintenance page instead of the usual website. Its notice attributed the precautionary suspension to a critical PHP vulnerability. The same page said its physical locations remained open and events were continuing as scheduled.<sup><a href="#ref-25">25</a></sup>

Calling FabCafe “down” would lose the most useful distinction. The website was unavailable at that observation; the operator said the cafés were operating. Neither statement dates the website's outage onset or proves what happened between our checks.

A security response involves several kinds of work: understanding local exposure, correcting advice, helping people act and assigning what remains. Those records matter. **Recovery asks a further question: which function can people use again, for whom, and since when?**

## A full row is not a successful recovery

The PHP row combines Sakura's distinction between provider-managed and customer-managed software, a community correction to the release schedule, and May First's maintenance notice and member guidance. No single organisation completed four measured stages. The last column records evidence of remaining work; a filled cell can describe something unfinished.

The chart tells us which kinds of evidence we found. More searching or more public documentation can fill more cells. It cannot tell us that PHP was handled best, or even that it was the most documented incident generally. Clustering similar rows makes the records easier to compare; it does not remove that selection bias.

The work itself is still important. Debian's xz advisory distinguished stable releases from affected development branches, making a broad warning locally useful.<sup><a href="#ref-5">5</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-6">6</a></sup> Apache explicitly explained why earlier Log4j mitigations were insufficient; its archived guidance retained class removal as a fallback, rather than discrediting every remedy.<sup><a href="#ref-10">10</a></sup> These are useful contributions, not measurements of how many users became protected.

Heartbleed supplies stronger action evidence. Researchers sent contacts lists of vulnerable hosts and recovery guidance. After eight days, 39.5% of early-notified contacts had at least one listed host patched, versus 26.8% of those not yet notified.<sup><a href="#ref-14">14</a></sup> That is a measured contact-level difference of 12.7 percentage points. It is neither a percentage of fully secured hosts nor a trial showing that assistance beats a general warning.

May First's PHP notice shows another form of help: it reports patching its shared webservers, including older PHP, and links instructions while encouraging members to change versions.<sup><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup> Its maintenance commitment is qualified by members' needs and its capability.<sup><a href="#ref-17">17</a></sup> We can describe the help without claiming that every member followed it or that every site was recovered.

## The website and the service can recover separately

We therefore ran a separate, bounded Japan pilot: twelve provider–function pairs across internet access, banking/payments, energy, flights, rail and messaging/comms, for 20 October–3 November. Official news came first; notices guided endpoint checks and archive lookups. Providers without usable evidence stayed in the sample.

Three units yielded scoped historical operator reports: DOCOMO voice services, TEPCO electricity supply in part of Chiba, and JR East train operations on some Chiba lines.<sup><a href="#ref-29">29</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-30">30</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-31">31</a></sup> Nine lacked adequate evidence for the selected function. None of the twelve had an independently verified present function state. **Unknown did not mean unavailable—or normal.** The retained pilot notices did not explicitly establish a PHP link; this is not evidence that PHP caused no disruption.

The function boundary changes the reading. Mitsubishi UFJ NICOS reported returning its members-only WEB login page to its normal presentation from 26 October and resuming some functions. It did not individually name corporate purchasing-card statement retrieval.<sup><a href="#ref-27">27</a></sup> That notice cannot settle whether statements were available, still less whether card payments worked.

Kansai Transmission & Distribution reported suspending website functions, including part of its outage-information service, and restoring them in a same-date notice on 22 October.<sup><a href="#ref-28">28</a></sup> An app was offered as an alternative information channel. These are records of website disruption and recovery, not electricity-supply failure and restoration.

Even a “normal” label needs checking. Some inspected pages contained hidden or default status templates populated by JavaScript. Reading their unrendered HTML as a live all-clear would have created false normality in our results.

## The first archived error is not the start of an outage

For a named endpoint, we look for the last observed success, first observed failure, last observed failure and first observed recovery. Those observations can bound transitions. They do not prove continuous downtime between captures, and a missing notice is not a successful service test.

Operator reports can provide better clocks. DOCOMO describes a scoped voice disruption on 28 October from around 16:30 to around 17:29 JST.<sup><a href="#ref-29">29</a></sup> That supports a reported interval of roughly an hour, not an independently measured 59-minute outage for every subscriber. Its separate Okinawa restoration notice warns that temporary recovery could still leave some areas with difficulty or slower data speeds.<sup><a href="#ref-32">32</a></sup>

Archives helped recover JR East's notices, but not a complete service history. Two sampled pages carried a weather-related train-suspension banner. A separate software-related digital-services notice appeared in the later sample and expressly excluded train-operation effects.<sup><a href="#ref-31">31</a></sup> The observations must not be joined into one PHP outage or one continuous duration.

## Stopping a service does not settle containment

Binance's initial account of its 29 October incident adds a separate example. It says suspending withdrawals and shutting hot-wallet services did not invalidate malicious authorisations already recorded on-chain. The team needed a separate procedure to revoke those permissions.<sup><a href="#ref-26">26</a></sup>

That is Binance's initial account, published on 3 November, not an independent forensic finding. It does not establish a PHP connection or a time when normal customer withdrawal service was restored. The operational distinction is important: a service can be suspended while a compromised capability remains usable. Containment, restoring customer access and establishing security require different evidence. This case is not included in either of the two samples above.

## Different functions of one warning finished days apart

PHP's release was announced for 23:00 in Japan on 21 October.<sup><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup> Pixiv took pixiv, Encyclopedia and FANBOX offline at 19:00 JST and reopened at 23:30 after applying updates and finishing checks.<sup><a href="#ref-21">21</a></sup> Asken stopped its apps, website and corporate services around 22:50 and restored all of them at 09:25 the next morning.<sup><a href="#ref-22">22</a></sup> In this pair the earlier closure also reopened earlier, and neither record shows how patching was split from testing or whether either service could have stayed online safely.

Korean FFXIV puts the split inside one response. New logins returned on 23 October after 25 hours 25 minutes; settings backup stayed restricted until 26 October at 18:08, about three days and 15 hours later.<sup><a href="#ref-23">23</a></sup><sup><a href="#ref-24">24</a></sup> Two functions of one warning, with two endpoints.

## If you run the systems, report the function you checked

Keep the notice you acted on, its version and the time. If advice changes, preserve what it replaced. A current page may no longer show the instructions someone followed earlier.

Find the person who controls each layer before the next warning. The build pipeline, the configuration file, the managed database and the customer's application may sit with different people.

Route a practical obstacle to someone who can address it: a vendor, distributor, colleague or support community. State the boundary of that help rather than assuming a published procedure was used successfully.

Report the checkpoints separately: patch installed, permissions revoked, harmful activity contained, function restored, exceptions remaining. For restoration, name the function, affected users or region, observation time and evidence. A successful webpage load cannot substitute for a transaction or delivery check; neither alone proves that a compromised capability has been removed.

Distinguish a workaround from restoration of the original channel. Say when an outcome is operator-reported, directly observed or still unknown. Missing completion evidence is not proof that no work happened.

## Ask what people can do again

The next useful sentence after “we responded” names something a person can do again. Can they retrieve the statement, reach the service or use the original channel? If we cannot yet answer, that is a gap to investigate—not a box to colour as complete.

<details>
<summary>How the sample was chosen</summary>

Twenty incidents were screened against one evidence rule: nineteen qualified, and one was excluded. The retained MOVEit 2023 pack lacked a qualifying vendor or operator account, and three reserve cases remain unscreened. Five cases were inherited from an earlier study, and five were added in each of three lanes: broad disclosure, active exploitation and supply-chain trust.

A case needed three distinct events and two primary documents. That rule selects well-documented, mostly English-language and famous episodes, so the rows show mechanisms rather than frequency, national capability or response quality. Most retained records are disclosure, guidance, assessment or correction; only a minority report an action or an outcome, and no new case independently verifies installed protection. The sample cannot test warning volume, lead time, or the effect of either.

The nineteen cases: Shellshock (2014), DROWN (2016), KRACK (2017), Meltdown and Spectre (2018), OpenSSL (2022), WannaCry (2017), NotPetya (2017), ProxyLogon (2021), Citrix Bleed (2023), DigiNotar (2011), SolarWinds (2020), Codecov (2021), ua-parser-js (2021), 3CX (2023), PHP (2026), eMudhra (2026), Log4Shell (2021), xz (2024) and Heartbleed (2014).

<details>
<summary>The separate Japan outcome pilot</summary>

The twelve preselected provider–function pairs covered NTT East and SoftBank fixed broadband; LINE message delivery and DOCOMO mobile voice/SMS; NICOS corporate purchasing-card statements and SMBC Direct online banking; TEPCO and Kansai electricity supply; ANA and JAL domestic flight operations; and JR East passenger trains and JR Central's Tokaido Shinkansen. This was a purposeful, Japan-first pilot, not a national sample. Reports about part of a function do not establish its whole scope; the DOCOMO sources, for example, do not establish SMS outcomes.

The bounded pass retained 69 direct GET attempts and 33 archive requests, including 21 exact-URL CDX queries. It inventoried 55 capture rows and attempted ten replays. These are collection counts, not outages, users affected or independent incidents. Access walls, collector TLS failures, query forms, client-rendered pages and incomplete archive coverage left substantial gaps. An archive's own error response is not necessarily the original site's error. No authenticated transaction, message delivery or physical-service test was performed; no continuous monitoring was started.

Event time, notice publication, archive capture and retrieval time are separate fields. The method is intended to transfer through a different roster, language, timezone and incident definition; only this Japan application has been exercised. The archive pass also recorded a policy-check error: it initially reused a robots policy across archive origins. A later correct-origin check does not retroactively repair that deviation.

The separately checked FabCafe homepage and Binance's new initial account are illustrative additions to the article, not additions to the nineteen-incident heatmap or the twelve-function pilot. Nothing from private correspondence is used here.
